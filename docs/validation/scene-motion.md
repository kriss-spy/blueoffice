# Workstation motion and quiet café reactions (#19)

Motion is a presentation controller over current office state. It cannot submit commands, answer requests or change work/lifecycle/session state. Compatible ready assistants use a short walk between their standing slot and approach slot; actual working state targets the standing slot before applying the independently reviewed compatible seated pose. Optional lounge behavior targets the reserved safe standing area and starts OFF. It never labels an idle assistant as doing off-screen work.

## Geometry and admission

`shared/scene-navigation.ts` derives obstacles from saved rotated workstation footprints and fixed café/plant furniture. Routes use a deterministic 20 cm cardinal grid with straight-segment checks every 5 cm and 16 cm clearance. The owned workstation remains blocked except its measured right standing aisle and front approach aisle; a route cannot cut across its desk or chair. Rotation carries these local aisles with the workstation. Other assistants reserve their actual location and interaction destination; active routes reserve their sampled corridor too. Stable assistant ID order resolves conflicts. An occupied, blocked or incompatible route returns an explicit stationary diagnostic.

`SceneMotionController` revalidates before advancing and whenever furniture/destination changes. Changed or removed desks cannot retain an old walk or seated target. Pending attention, stale/disconnected state and layout editing override movement immediately in render; this does not wait for the 50 ms presentation timer or pathfinding. Reduced motion and hidden/failing graphics also use safe stationary presentation. Inactive actors do not repeatedly update React state. Heading follows the route while the final work pose retains the measured desk rotation and seating profile.

Only explicit mapped clips that exist in an admitted reviewed pack may walk or react. Missing walk uses stationary presentation; missing reaction preserves Completed text without pretending to celebrate. The existing real-turn `CompletionTracker` suppresses initial history, replay and attention-consumed completions. The Avatar action resets for a new cue key and runs `LoopOnce`, once. Each avatar retains its independent clone/mixer and existing disposal contract. Static seating arrival and attention fallback remain immediate pose changes; no sit/get-up animation is invented.

## Sound provenance

`src/scene/scene-sound.ts` is original BlueOffice audio generated by two sine oscillators (660 and 880 Hz), with short envelopes and peak gain 0.025. It uses no samples, third-party sound files or redistributed character audio. The checkbox starts OFF on every page load and enabling it resumes AudioContext in that user action. Sound is triggered only for a newly observed real completion or exact attention request. Replayed requests, a lounge toggle and history do not notify; reduced motion and hidden state suppress notifications. Audio failure leaves visual attention available. Oscillators/gains disconnect on end and the context closes on unmount. Subjective speaker/headphone loudness is not an automated acceptance claim.

## Evidence and limits

Five focused unit scenarios cover rotated/fixed furniture, occupied slots/corridors, missing mapped walk, attention/reduced preemption, edited destinations, repeated inputs and owned workstation aisles in all four rotations. Browser scenarios in `scene-motion.spec.ts` use an original stationary triangle pack with explicitly reviewed engineering tracks. They establish movement/arrival without model commands, real `tool` state retained, immediate question preemption, edited desk validation, missing walk/reaction, replayed completion suppression, reduced motion and default-off sound with exactly one new attention notification. The triangle deliberately cannot establish gait, face or material quality.
